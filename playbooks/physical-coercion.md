# Physical coercion response

Physical safety takes priority over assets. During immediate danger, take the safest available path and contact emergency services when safe. This guidance cannot predict an attacker's behavior.

## During an incident

- Avoid confrontation or actions that could escalate violence. Do not require refusal to disclose access or require use of a decoy.
- Do not invent technical limitations such as a hardware-wallet warm-up period. Decoys, delays, and covert signals may increase danger if discovered.
- Use a pre-agreed emergency signal only if it is safe. You do not need a completed security setup to seek help.
- Follow emergency responders' directions when contact becomes possible. Collect evidence only when safe.

## After reaching safety

Seek medical attention and contact local law enforcement. In the United States, call 911 for an emergency. Coordinate further communication and evidence preservation with investigators. Preserve transaction hashes, addresses, timestamps, messages, and any safe observations.

From an independently trusted device, assess exposed keys, accounts, and delegated authority using the [wallet decision tree](wallet-compromise.md). Do not automatically revoke everything, add gas, or rush a migration from a device the attacker accessed.

## Planning before an incident

Establish trusted contacts, safe check-ins, family support, recovery access, and an emergency plan. Test communication without activating a real emergency response. Review exposure in [Domain 03](../03-opsec-physical-security/README.md).

Geographic signer separation and tested delays can constrain specific transfer paths. They do not prevent threats against relatives, prolonged coercion, or alternate authority paths. Decoys are optional and require individual risk assessment with qualified physical-security advice. They cannot establish a maximum coercion loss.
